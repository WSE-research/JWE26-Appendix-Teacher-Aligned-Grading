## A.2.1 Few-shot grading and comparison inventory

The ICWE paper placed its few-shot mechanism among approaches that adapt language-model graders to teacher expectations.

Several approaches leverage teacher-derived supervision: Chu et al. optimize teacher-guideline prompts iteratively to improve scoring agreement [\[4\]](references.md#ref-Chu2025GradeOpt), Woodrow et al. align generated student feedback via Direct Preference Optimization on teacher preferences [\[23\]](references.md#ref-Woodrow2025DPO), and Zhao et al. demonstrate that few-shot in-context examples improve LLM grading [\[24\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGrader).

In that study, instructor-graded responses to the same question reduced the absolute grading error of three OpenAI models compared with a prompt without examples, pooled over three public short-answer benchmarks with scores normalized to percentages, and retrieving the most similar graded responses produced a statistically significant further reduction for two of the three models [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1).[^1] In their few-shot conditions, Zhao et al. use five graded examples per prompt and note that a larger number increases computational cost [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1). The ICWE paper made this number the object of study: it varied the number of teacher-rated examples per prompt from zero to forty in a fold-wise sweep and reported for each model the smallest number with which it reached target levels of teacher-aligned accuracy [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE).

## A.2.2 Rubric conditioning and partial credit

In their few-shot conditions, Zhao et al. combine generic grading instructions with five human-graded responses to the same question, selected at random or by embedding distance, and ask for a 0–100 grade with feedback [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1). ([inspected versions](references.md#inspected-versions)) In a separate experiment on induction proofs, they replace the holistic judgment with seven binary rubric decisions, which improved accuracy significantly for two of four questions and had negligible influence on the other two [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1). Deng et al. call their pipeline rubric-conditioned, but the prompt contains the question, a reference answer, the student answer and an instruction naming the benchmark’s label categories, not question-specific weighted criteria [\[6\]](references.md#ref-Deng2025RubricConditioned). SAS-Bench supplies question-specific full scores, reference answers, candidate error causes and scoring rules; removing the guidelines degraded most of its metrics, and five human-scored examples improved the aggregate metrics of selected weaker models but, for some subjects, lowered a consistency score combining overall and step scores while quadratic weighted kappa (QWK) rose [\[12\]](references.md#ref-Lai2025SASBenchV2). ([inspected versions](references.md#inspected-versions)) Korthals et al. prompt with an example solution and a subtractive rubric [\[11\]](references.md#ref-Korthals2025Canvas), and LaTA applies an instructor-authored rubric of binary items with a reference solution [\[19\]](references.md#ref-Rodriguez2026LaTA). In this article, all grading prompts reuse the teacher-rated in-context examples of the ICWE protocol [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE); the partial-credit runs use a generic prompt that defines three levels (wrong or missing, partially correct, fully correct) rather than an expert rubric, and their in-context examples keep the binary teacher label (Section 4.4). The independent human reference (Section 4.2) is used only for evaluation and does not enter any prompt.

The returned score is also defined differently across these studies. Zhao et al. normalize the native scales of three benchmarks to percentages and report root-mean-square error (RMSE) and Pearson correlation [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1). Deng et al. compare 2-, 3- and 5-way schemes whose five-way codes (correct, contradictory, partially correct, irrelevant, non-domain) the authors treat as semantic categories unsuited to correlation; accuracy and Cohen’s kappa decline from the binary to the five-way scheme [\[6\]](references.md#ref-Deng2025RubricConditioned). That decline describes a change of label space on one benchmark, not a general property of partial credit. SAS-Bench scores whole answers and segmented steps on question-specific scales and combines both in a collaborative consistency score (CCS) [\[12\]](references.md#ref-Lai2025SASBenchV2). Korthals et al. award 0–1 points per task with fractional deductions [\[11\]](references.md#ref-Korthals2025Canvas), Grade Guard inherits a 0–5 reference in half points [\[5\]](references.md#ref-Dadu2025GradeGuard), and LaTA allows only full or zero points per item, expressing partial credit by splitting a step into several binary items [\[19\]](references.md#ref-Rodriguez2026LaTA). This article codes human partial credit on a raw scale from 0 to 4 and analyzes it through a fixed map (0 to 0; 1, 2 and 3 to 1; 4 to 2) (Section 4.5 of the article). Because the map merges three levels, agreement on the raw and on the mapped scale are separate views, and the lost resolution is a property of the mapping rather than a finding about the graders. Binary grades are compared with the same reference after a third transformation, which counts an answer as correct from raw level 3 and is varied as a sensitivity analysis (Section 4.5 of the article).

## A.2.3 Human references and instructor involvement


The reference against which agreement is computed is part of the measurement design, and the reviewed studies construct it differently. For its three public short-answer benchmarks, Zhao et al. use the inherited instructor grades without describing how they were rated or reconciled; only for its separate proof dataset do the authors name the seven-item rubric and point to high inter-rater reliability in earlier work [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1). Deng et al. likewise use the labels shipped with SciEntsBank without describing their rating protocol [\[6\]](references.md#ref-Deng2025RubricConditioned). Grade Guard reuses a dataset in which the average of two independent evaluators, rounded to half points, serves as the reference [\[5\]](references.md#ref-Dadu2025GradeGuard).

In arXiv v2, SAS-Bench divides 18 experts into an annotation and a verification group and resolves significant discrepancies by discussion and, where needed, re-annotation [\[12\]](references.md#ref-Lai2025SASBenchV2). That version describes its responses as simulated by language models and identifies the resulting distribution shift as a limitation [\[12\]](references.md#ref-Lai2025SASBenchV2). In Korthals et al., the instructor and three doctoral students graded without seeing the model’s grades, but the paper does not report double coding of each answer [\[11\]](references.md#ref-Korthals2025Canvas). Sunkavalli contrasts a Portuguese essay corpus with one pooled score vector per essay and no rater identities with an English corpus that has two independent trained raters per essay, whose mutual error serves as the accuracy benchmark [\[21\]](references.md#ref-Sunkavalli2026JudgesAsRaters). These procedures differ in independence from model output, duplicate rating and handling of disagreement, and a missing description does not show that a procedure was absent upstream. The predecessor audit of this system converted each reviewer’s decision on a label proposed by an operational grader into a binary label and therefore treated the majority of these labels as a pragmatic, possibly anchored reference [\[9\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICALT). The binary teacher label of the ICWE pool has the same origin: it is the decision of the course’s operational auto-grader, kept where the lecturer confirmed it and inverted where the lecturer overruled it, although the ICWE paper describes it only as manual review data [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE). For RQ3 of the article, both authors coded all 439 distinct question–answer texts independently, without access to the historical grade or to any model output, and archived their agreement before any discussion (Section 4.2). They then jointly adjudicated all 71 texts whose raw levels differed by two or more levels, which left 152 texts with adjacent levels. The reference is therefore adjudicated for distances of two or more levels, with the adjacent disagreements retained as residual dissent. Because its coders are the authors, it is model-blind but not naive about the study (Section 6.3 of the article). This is a protocol difference from the operational label, not evidence of an error-free reference.

Other studies place humans inside the operational grading process instead of producing an independent reference. Avalon calibrates a GPT-4o grading agent through independent grading of the same submissions by instructor and agent, then lets students self-grade without seeing the agent’s scores and dispute mismatches, which instructors and teaching assistants review [\[2\]](references.md#ref-Armfield2025AvalonManuscript). For 391 submissions comprising 1,332 rubric items assessed by both the agent and the students, rubric-level alignment between self-grades and agent scores was 78%; after the students had compared their self-assessments with the agent’s evaluation, 93% of their evaluations agreed with it, and the authors note that both may agree while being wrong [\[2\]](references.md#ref-Armfield2025AvalonManuscript). ([inspected versions](references.md#inspected-versions)) Korthals et al. had reviewed 210 of the 1,248 disagreements between the model and blind course graders at the time of writing, and surveyed 30 students on the feedback [\[11\]](references.md#ref-Korthals2025Canvas). LaTA counts instructor-confirmed regrade requests and states that this channel is weakest at detecting missed deductions, because students have no incentive to report undeserved credit [\[19\]](references.md#ref-Rodriguez2026LaTA). Agreement after exposure, appeal counts, sampled reviews and perceived helpfulness thus describe how people interact with a grading system; they do not estimate accuracy on all items or learning effects. The teacher review interface and feedback store studied here are inherited from the ICWE design [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE); this article evaluates offline and does not measure self-assessment, appeals, feedback use, teacher workload or learning.

## A.2.4 Reliability, deployment identity and rationales

Selective automation and repeated sampling are often reported together but address different properties. Deng et al. query their grader ten times per answer, accept a label only under a sufficient majority, and report retained-subset accuracy together with coverage; they also qualitatively inspect selected model reasoning in Section 4.5 and Appendix B of the inspected preprint [\[6\]](references.md#ref-Deng2025RubricConditioned). Grade Guard uses the normalized standard deviation of ten sampled scores as an indecisiveness score and forwards answers above a tuned threshold to a human evaluator [\[5\]](references.md#ref-Dadu2025GradeGuard), without reporting what happened to the forwarded answers. Sunkavalli separates dependability from accuracy: every judge reaches a dependability of at least .80 with one or two replications, yet the best judge beats the error between two trained raters on only one of four English essay sets [\[21\]](references.md#ref-Sunkavalli2026JudgesAsRaters). An accepted-set metric is therefore interpretable only with its acceptance rule and denominator, and stable scores can still disagree with the human reference. Confidence gating, coverage and calibration of this grading component were analyzed in the predecessor papers [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE), [\[9\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICALT) and are not presented as new here. This article uses repeated runs to quantify repeat discordance for the serving comparison and to assess the stability of feedback-threshold attainment; repeat discordance is the number of items on which one configuration disagrees with itself across two runs, which sets the noise scale against which the serving comparison is read (Section 4.5 of the article). It reports coverage for the decidable sets of the rationale instruments and for the referral policy of the decision component, each with its own denominator (Sections 4.3 and 5.1.2).

The identity of the deployed model is a further condition of any reported agreement. Sunkavalli finds that all five version contrasts among twelve judges on one hosted platform shift severity beyond a permutation null while agreement with the human reference also moves in four of them, so the registered hypothesis of a silent drift fails [\[21\]](references.md#ref-Sunkavalli2026JudgesAsRaters). Because the judges are hosted aliases, the author reads each contrast as a joint change of weights and serving stack and names quantization and batching as differences the design cannot separate [\[21\]](references.md#ref-Sunkavalli2026JudgesAsRaters). LaTA grades with one locally hosted open-weight model, using a second local model only for segmentation, and states that it does not compare its grading decisions with those of a cloud-hosted model, another local model or a teaching assistant on the same submissions [\[19\]](references.md#ref-Rodriguez2026LaTA). Neither study therefore shows whether the same weights behave alike when served through different paths. The ICWE evaluation called all of its models, the open-weight ones included, through one hosted API [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE), and the predecessor audit included a locally served open-weight model next to hosted models, but not the same model in both conditions [\[9\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICALT). Within RQ1 of the article, the only prespecified primary test of this article compares one declared open-weight model variant, served locally with vLLM and requested under the same model name through the hosted API, against the teacher label, using an equivalence margin fixed in advance (Section 4.5 of the article). Its original run used temperature 0.2; after that result was known, plan amendment D10 added same-endpoint repeat controls and a rerun of both arms at temperature 0, and both runs are reported (Section 5.1.1). Before the ICWE results and the new results are set side by side, the five ICWE models are re-run under the harmonized settings of this article as a replication criterion (Section 5.1). Since matching model names do not establish parity of weights or quantization, the declared conditions are reported with the comparison.

Studies that evaluate explanations differ in what the explanation is compared with. SAS-Bench constrains models to choose error causes from a predefined list and compares their frequency distribution with expert annotations within score intervals, an errors consistency score (ECS) that the authors present as a test of explainability [\[12\]](references.md#ref-Lai2025SASBenchV2). Korthals et al. read the model’s step-by-step thoughts alongside the human graders’ explanations to classify disagreements and suggest that issues flagged in those thoughts likely explain some deductions outside the rubric [\[11\]](references.md#ref-Korthals2025Canvas). LaTA separates an audit rationale for staff from an answer-withholding hint for students and reports occasional leakage of reference-solution content into feedback, without validating either text against a reference [\[19\]](references.md#ref-Rodriguez2026LaTA). Zhao et al. leave the quality of generated feedback, including its relationship to the grades, to future work [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1), as Grade Guard does for rationales [\[5\]](references.md#ref-Dadu2025GradeGuard). Gur-Arieh et al. define a reasoning step as faithful when it accurately describes a process that took place in the model, separate this from plausibility and causal importance, and find that most existing faithfulness metrics perform near chance on tasks constructed to reveal the underlying process [\[8\]](references.md#ref-GurArieh2026Faithfulness). Agreement with expert error codes, perceived usefulness, process faithfulness and agreement between a rationale and its own score thus require different references, and a rationale compatible with its score verifies neither the answer nor the process behind the score. RQ4 of the article addresses only the last property: whether the judgment expressed in a grader’s rationale agrees with the score the grader recorded. It reads each rationale with a fixed lexicon rule, a sensitivity variant of that rule and two rationale-only language-model judges that receive the rationale and fixed categories but not the score, the question, the separate answer text or the source model (Section 4.3). Because these instruments decide different subsets of rationales and no human rationale corpus exists for this course, each instrument is reported with its coverage and on a common denominator, and consistency is not read as correctness, faithfulness or feedback quality.

## A.2.5 Model-backed grading as a web service

When a grader runs as a web service, its reliability also depends on service-level mechanisms. Saleh Sedghpour et al. show experimentally that retry and circuit-breaker policies of a service mesh affect microservice performance and robustness, and that retries combined with a sensitive circuit breaker can produce retry storms [\[20\]](references.md#ref-SalehSedghpour2022ServiceMesh). Gomes et al. map observability research for microservice applications, with traces, metrics and logs as its main data types [\[7\]](references.md#ref-Gomes2025Observability). For hosted language models, Chen et al. found substantial behavior changes of the same service between two releases three months apart and argue for continuous monitoring [\[3\]](references.md#ref-Chen2024Drift). The ICWE architecture records request identifiers, prompt versions, parse outcomes, retries and decisions for traceability, and its model-agnostic adapter is meant to let providers be replaced and the evaluation protocol be re-executed without changing the surrounding web application [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE). This article does not evaluate service-level robustness. It re-executes that protocol after components behind the adapter have changed, and Section 6.2 of the article discusses which grading evidence a web system has to re-establish after such a change, a question that the recorded traces make answerable but do not answer.

## A.2.6 Decision components and selective escalation

As the exploratory component substitution within RQ1 of the article, Section 5.1.2 replaces the language-model grader with a commercial decision model, Jev by TypeSafe, which answers typed questions with label probabilities under a fixed output contract, so that uncertain cases can be escalated. Learning to defer treats a predictor and a downstream expert as one system in which the expert may also err [\[16\]](references.md#ref-Mozannar2020Defer). In ASAG, Li et al. learn a deferral policy over three correctness levels and evaluate it by retrieving the correct label whenever the policy defers [\[15\]](references.md#ref-Li2023DeferASAG), and CHiL(L)Grader routes low-confidence answers to teachers after temperature scaling and simulates their corrections with ground-truth grades [\[17\]](references.md#ref-Raikote2026CHiLLGrader). Constrained decoding can guarantee the structure of an output [\[22\]](references.md#ref-Willard2023GuidedGeneration), which leaves the correctness of an admissible label to be evaluated separately. Two recent preprints evaluate the Jev decision model used here on other workloads: Rao and Callison-Burch find that on ordinal rubric criteria Jev and three language-model judges depart from the human raters together, and that no cascade from Jev to such a judge beats the best single judge by more than 2.7 points, even with oracle thresholds [\[18\]](references.md#ref-Rao2026JEVRubricJudges). Li et al. accept confident Jev verdicts and escalate the rest to a reasoning judge: under a threshold frozen in advance, this cascade improved on the reasoning judge on pooled held-out preference pairs but was slightly less accurate on the JudgeBench subset; a prospective live test on two new workloads matched the judge with thresholds calibrated separately for each workload; and confidence routing weakened on style-adversarial pairs [\[14\]](references.md#ref-Li2026JEVAsJudge). These results concern rubric judging and preference evaluation rather than this course’s lecturer-reviewed operational whole-answer binary label, and deferral evaluated with retrieved or simulated labels does not measure review effort. The comparison in this article is therefore exploratory: it uses the operational binary teacher label, reports accepted-case agreement with coverage for the referral policy, and simulates the cascade from saved grader outputs, without a risk guarantee or a measured saving.
