## A.3.1 Additional requirement rationale

The labels L1–L4 below identify four extension needs derived from the ICWE study and its experimental design [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE). They are local shorthand for the rationale in this appendix; the accompanying article states the requirements without these labels.

| Label | Extension need |
|---|---|
| L1 | The predecessor evaluated binary correctness only; partial credit needs a reference on its own scale. |
| L2 | The pool came from one course and one teacher, with operational grader decisions confirmed or inverted in lecturer review. It was not independently graded from scratch. |
| L3 | All five predecessor models used one hosted API, so the design did not compare local and hosted serving paths for the same nominal open-weight model. This need follows from the design rather than from the predecessor's stated limitations. |
| L4 | Rankings were tied to an evaluation date, and the protocol was intended to be rerun when models change. This article interprets re-evaluation as requiring traceable conditions, repeated-call stability and suitable evidence for any extended output. |

Each of the following six requirements answers at least one of these limitations, and the related work in Section 2 of the article shows how the corresponding property is handled elsewhere. The requirements constrain the evaluation design; they do not prescribe a grading method.

1.  **R1** *Explicit reference per property.* Every property on which a deployment relies is evaluated against a named reference that is defined on the scale of the grading output, and agreement with a reference, stability across repeated calls, and consistency between a rationale and its score are reported as separate quantities. This requirement answers L1 and L2. On the response pool of this article, the lecturer confirmed the operational grader on 407 of the 450 rows and overruled it on 43, so agreement with the teacher label is to a large part agreement with the operational grader. Related studies likewise evaluate against inherited benchmark labels whose rating procedure is not reported [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1), [\[6\]](references.md#ref-Deng2025RubricConditioned), against pooled scores without rater identities, or against two independent trained raters [\[21\]](references.md#ref-Sunkavalli2026JudgesAsRaters), so a result is interpretable only together with its reference (Section 2.2). In this article, the teacher label, the independent partial-credit reference, and the grader’s own recorded score are therefore three distinct references, each tied to its own research question.

2.  **R2** *Independent construction of new references.* A reference for a new output scale is produced by human coders who see neither model outputs nor the operational decision, the agreement between their independent codings is archived before any disagreement is discussed, and disagreements that remain unresolved are reported instead of being averaged away. L1 makes a new reference necessary, because a binary label cannot validate the distinctions within a partial-credit scale, and L2 rules out building it as another review of proposed decisions, because a reviewer who sees a proposal can be anchored by it [\[9\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICALT). Avalon reports two agreement values that differ in what the students had seen: an overall alignment of 78% between students’ self-grades and the grading agent’s rubric scores, and 93% agreement with the agent’s judgment after the students had compared their self-assessments with the agent’s evaluations [\[2\]](references.md#ref-Armfield2025AvalonManuscript). The comparison does not isolate an effect of exposure, but it illustrates why an agreement value should be read together with what the raters had seen. Conversely, a reference formed as the rounded average of two independent evaluators [\[5\]](references.md#ref-Dadu2025GradeGuard) keeps no trace of the disagreement between them.

3.  **R3** *Matched comparison with explicit denominators.* Configurations before and after a change are evaluated on the same items with the same in-context examples, prompt, and declared decoding settings, and every result states its unit of analysis, its coverage, and its missing or abstained cases. This requirement answers L3: a contrast between model families says nothing about the serving path, and Sunkavalli points out that contrasts between hosted endpoints bundle the weights with the serving stack, so that separating the two requires pinned open-weight checkpoints [\[21\]](references.md#ref-Sunkavalli2026JudgesAsRaters). Matching includes the decoding settings: in this study, the locally served arm initially ran with a larger output-token limit and without the constrained response format of the hosted arm, which would have confounded the serving path with constrained decoding until both arms were harmonized. Denominators matter as well, since agreement on an accepted subset is only meaningful together with the share of cases the subset covers [\[6\]](references.md#ref-Deng2025RubricConditioned), and this article counts 450 model rows but 439 unique question–answer texts (Section 4.1).

4.  **R4** *Stability baseline.* A difference between two configurations is interpreted against the variation between repeated runs of one configuration at the same operating point, and results obtained at different operating points are not pooled. The ICWE evaluation used a single repetition per condition and named repeated runs as the way to strengthen the stability evidence for close model pairs [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE); a re-execution after a model change (L4) and an equivalence claim about two serving paths (L3) face the same problem. Stability is nonetheless no substitute for agreement: in Sunkavalli’s study, every judge reaches a dependability of at least .80 with one or two replications, yet the best judge beats the error between two trained raters on only one of four English essay sets [\[21\]](references.md#ref-Sunkavalli2026JudgesAsRaters).

5.  **R5** *Score-blind reading of rationales.* Whether a rationale agrees with its score is decided by instruments that do not receive the score, each instrument reports which rationales it cannot decide, and instruments are compared on the rationales that they all decide. Under the reading of L4 above, a re-executed protocol may extend the generated output, and a rationale is the extension that a review interface would display next to the grade; an instrument that sees the score could align its reading with it. Related work compares explanations with different references: expert error causes [\[12\]](references.md#ref-Lai2025SASBenchV2), process faithfulness, which Gur-Arieh et al. distinguish from plausibility and for which most of the metrics they tested perform near chance on their constructed benchmark [\[8\]](references.md#ref-GurArieh2026Faithfulness), or no reference at all, where Zhao et al. leave the quality of generated feedback and its relation to the grades to future work [\[25\]](references.md#ref-Zhao2025FewShotGraderV1) and Grade Guard leaves the generation of rationales for its grades to future work [\[5\]](references.md#ref-Dadu2025GradeGuard). For the course of this article, no human rationale corpus exists (a lecturer comment is present on 71 of the 7,491 rows of the assessment), so the grader’s own recorded score is the only available reference, and consistency with it shows neither correctness nor faithfulness.

6.  **R6** *Traceable records.* Every evaluated decision is linked to the model identifier returned by the backend, the serving path, the prompt version, the decoding settings, the in-context examples, and the raw input and output, so that a re-evaluation can be repeated and its gaps can be named. It makes the re-execution named in L4 checkable, since the behavior of a hosted model can change between releases while its name stays the same [\[3\]](references.md#ref-Chen2024Drift). The ICWE logging layer records request identifiers, prompt versions, parse outcomes, retries, confidence values, and final decisions [\[10\]](references.md#ref-Gwozdz2026ICWE), but the ICWE runs recorded only alias identifiers of the hosted models, so whether a model changed between the ICWE run and its re-execution cannot be checked. A declared setting can also fail silently: in this study, a provenance sample for one hosted model name revealed 16 upstream providers, and five of its 1,800 retained responses contained billed reasoning tokens despite the declared setting, all five from one provider, which only the per-call token records revealed.

## A.3.2 Output-contract validation detail

The following detail concerns output-contract change (b) among the four extensions in Section 3.3 of the article.

*(b) Output contract.* The second change extends the output of the grading service. In the rationale condition, the model returns a short rationale in a field `reason` next to `correct` and `confidence`; in the partial-credit condition, it returns an integer `level` from 0 to 2 next to `correct` and `confidence`. Unlike changes (a) and (c), this change does not pass the adapter boundary unnoticed: the output validation, the review display, and the analysis have to handle the new field, and the field can be lost silently where they do not. In this study, the evaluation pipeline at first extracted only `correct` and `confidence` from the raw responses, and its binary coercion would have turned every level-2 grade into a missing value had the partial-credit level been read through it. An extended output also needs evidence on its own terms: a partial-credit level needs a reference on its scale (R1, R2), and a rationale needs an instrument that reads it without the score (R5). The in-context examples stay binary in both conditions, and the partial-credit prompt states the level definitions without an expert rubric, so the extended output is not adapted to the teacher in the way the binary decision is. This change addresses L1 for the partial-credit level and, under the reading of L4 in [A.3.1](03-design.md#a31), the extension of the output by a rationale; it is examined in RQ3 of the article and RQ4 of the article.
